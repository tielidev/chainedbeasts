## Why do simple movement calls matter?

Two individually sensible routes can conflict when players are connected. Before passing an obstacle, say which side you intend to use. If someone is recovering position or picking up equipment, give them time to respond instead of continuing to pull.

Think of a call as a shared decision rather than an order. A teammate may see a hazard or enemy you have missed. The [multiplayer guide](/co-op/multiplayer-guide/) covers preparation that makes those conversations easier.

## How can you use a chain offensively?

The store confirms that coordinated chains can trip enemies. Learn how this behaves in the tutorial or a low-pressure encounter before making a dangerous fight depend on it. We do not claim a universal trip threshold, damage value or boss interaction.

Our advice is to combine a chain attempt with a clear escape direction. If it does not work, the crew should know where to regroup rather than improvise in opposite directions. Review [combat fundamentals](/combat/combat-basics/) for the role of space and hazards.

## What changes after an affliction?

Effects that change movement, visibility or proximity can make yesterday's formation unreliable. The launch affliction preview includes Annoying, whose description specifically involves nearby allies. Read the current effect and discuss what must change.

The [afflictions guide](/combat/afflictions-and-healing/) explains why a recovery choice should be a team conversation. Do not assume a player is making random mistakes when a condition has changed the way they can act.

## What should you practice as a new crew?

Try moving around one obstacle together, regrouping after a thrown weapon and calling off an attack. These are our suggested exercises, not game objectives or guaranteed optimal tactics. The aim is a repeatable response when the arena becomes chaotic.

## What short calls can a new team agree on?

Choose a small vocabulary that describes intent. The examples below are suggested communication habits, not game commands. Agree on their meaning before the arena becomes noisy.

| Call | Shared response |
| --- | --- |
| “Left of the pillar” | Everyone considers the same side before passing |
| “Regroup” | Stop pursuing separate targets and choose open space |
| “Throwing” | Allies know that equipment and position may change |
| “Cancel” | Abandon the attempted interaction together |

The person making a call should still listen. An ally may already be committed to an animation or see an enemy outside your focus. Repeating a command more loudly does not remove that constraint. A short pause to agree on the next direction is often a better practice exercise than forcing a complicated chain attack repeatedly.

## How should you review a chain-related failure?

Reconstruct the sequence before assigning blame. Identify the first decision that separated the group, the obstacle or enemy that limited the return route, and the moment someone could no longer move as expected. Check for a new affliction before assuming the person ignored the plan.

Then change one part of the sequence. You might choose the other side of the obstacle, call the turn earlier, or stop chasing the distant target. The goal is a repeatable team response. This guide does not claim that chains trip every enemy, interrupt every attack or protect players from environmental damage; those interactions require their own evidence.
