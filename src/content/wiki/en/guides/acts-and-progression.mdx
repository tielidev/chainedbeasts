## Where does the campaign begin?

Carthage is the opening chapter and the setting used for the public Demo. Its role makes it a sensible place to learn the movement and environmental interactions described in our [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/). We do not assume the number or balance of Demo battles transfers unchanged to launch.

## What is different about Germania?

The developer's July beta announcement introduced Germania as Act 2, with a colder arena, new threats and spike traps. It also introduced Goliath, described as having jumping and ground-slam attacks. These are documented beta details, not an exhaustive launch boss moveset.

Use the announcement as context for why positioning matters. Do not infer exact health, weakness or damage values from promotional footage. Our [combat guide](/combat/combat-basics/) focuses on observing hazards and retaining space to react.

## What has been announced for Rome?

The September platform announcement identifies Rome as the final chapter and names Praefectus with the Praetorian Guard. It does not provide enough information for a verified attack-by-attack boss walkthrough, so we are not presenting one.

This distinction matters when planning a run: a complete route needs information beyond Carthage, while a first-run introduction can be useful without pretending to contain all late-game solutions.

## How do upgrades fit into progression?

Skills, equipment and accumulated injuries shape subsequent fights. The developer has also described surgery between acts. Review the [skills and shopping guide](/combat/skills-and-shopping/) and the [healing guide](/combat/afflictions-and-healing/) before making a costly decision. The launch announcement lists ten difficulty levels; four-level Demo references should be labeled as historical.

## How can you use the chapter structure to prepare a run?

Treat the route as a framework for planning, not a complete encounter order. The named locations identify the campaign's progression, while the details of individual fights still depend on the version and evidence available.

| Chapter | What the cited announcements establish | What this does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carthage | Opening chapter and public Demo setting | An unchanged launch encounter sequence |
| Germania | Act 2; the beta introduced Goliath and spike traps | Every launch enemy, arena layout or boss phase |
| Rome | Final chapter; Praefectus and the Praetorian Guard | A complete final-boss strategy |

Before moving on, review the tools you actually have. A team that relies on close formation should reconsider after a condition changes movement or camera behavior. A player who repeatedly throws their main weapon should make sure the next plan includes how to recover equipment. These are preparation recommendations, not stage-specific requirements.

## What should you record when sharing a route or walkthrough?

Include the act, installed build, platform, difficulty and whether the session is solo or cooperative. If a route comes from the Demo or beta, put that information near the claim rather than only in a footnote. A screenshot showing one arena cannot establish that every subsequent encounter follows the same sequence.

Separate “this appeared in my run” from “this always appears.” That distinction is essential before turning an observation into a full campaign checklist. The same care applies to surgery and difficulty progression: an announced system can be described without inventing prices, access conditions or unlock thresholds.
