## How many difficulty levels are announced?

Featherweight Games' launch overview lists ten increasingly difficult levels. It does not name each one, specify all modifiers or give a complete unlock sequence. A list of ten names or numerical enemy bonuses would therefore require evidence beyond that overview.

The campaign's three acts and nine battles before a final encounter describe its structure, not ten separate campaigns. Keep these counts separate when interpreting a feature list. The [campaign progression guide](/guides/acts-and-progression/) explains the named chapters and the limits of the available route information.

## Which difficulty names come from the Demo?

The June updated-Demo announcement named four options: Pleb, Legionnaire, Centurion and Hades. That is useful for identifying the version shown in an old video, but it does not establish that the full game has only those four levels or that every condition stayed unchanged.

If a guide says a particular boss victory unlocks the next setting, check which build its evidence concerns. An initial Demo announcement described higher difficulty after beating the Demo boss; that should not silently become a universal full-game unlock rule.

## Why can difficulty affect more than an enemy's health?

The developer's earlier training update describes a system difference: simpler training on Pleb and interactive training on higher settings, with a shared experience goal and the risk of individual injury. It demonstrates why difficulty advice should examine the actual systems that change rather than assume only a health multiplier.

That account is historical design documentation. It is not a tested launch threshold, a current repetition limit or permission to reuse old numerical training advice. Read the offer and prompts in your build. Our [skills and shopping guide](/combat/skills-and-shopping/) covers how to evaluate the consequence of an upgrade decision.

## How should a new group pick a setting?

Our recommendation is to choose a setting on which the party can learn one interaction at a time. First establish input control, a shared route around obstacles and a response when a throw or chain attempt fails. Raising difficulty while changing every player's beast and equipment makes it harder to identify what caused a failure.

Before changing settings, discuss the objective: learning an encounter, practicing coordination or attempting a harder completion. Check the installed game's description for the intended option. Do not choose around an assumed unlock reward, achievement condition or drop-rate bonus that the sources do not establish.

Afterward, review one concrete outcome, such as recovering position safely or keeping the party together. The [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) gives practice goals that do not depend on an unverified scaling formula.
