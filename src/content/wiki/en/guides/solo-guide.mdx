## What is confirmed about solo play?

The store identifies single-player alongside online and shared-screen co-op. It describes the same broad ingredients: weapons, environmental attacks, injuries and upgrades. The source does not establish a solo-only damage formula or an exact enemy-scaling rule, so neither is asserted here.

The full release's [campaign structure](/guides/acts-and-progression/) is distinct from the old Carthage Demo. Treat a video of the Demo as an illustration of mechanics, not proof that the whole launch campaign ends at its first boss.

## How should you prepare for a solo fight?

Our recommendation is to prioritize a route out of danger before a route to another hit. Look for weapons or objects you can reach after a throw and for hazards that can be turned against enemies. Avoid assuming that a large group can be safely attacked merely because each enemy is individually weak.

Use the tutorial to learn what your current defensive options actually do. The [combat basics guide](/combat/combat-basics/) offers a decision process rather than an invented invulnerability timing chart.

## How do you make recovery choices alone?

Read every affliction and skill description with your own next fight in mind. A drawback that teammates could help compensate for may be more disruptive alone. We suggest considering visibility, mobility and access to a usable weapon before selecting a benefit purely for damage.

The [afflictions guide](/combat/afflictions-and-healing/) explains the official health trade-off. This page does not claim that a specific beast is the best solo choice: the available evidence confirms unlocks and variants, not a tested ranking. Start with a setup you can understand and evaluate what actually ended your run.

## How should a solo player prepare for an unfamiliar encounter?

Our recommendation is to reduce simultaneous decisions. Learn how your current equipment behaves before adding an unfamiliar skill or variant. Watch the arena boundary and the space behind you when committing to an attack; a useful opening is one you can leave as well as enter.

| Situation | Question to ask before committing |
| --- | --- |
| An enemy is beside a hazard | Can I use the environment without trapping myself nearby? |
| A weapon can be thrown | What will I use if the target survives or the throw misses? |
| A recovery offer adds an affliction | Does this interfere with how I currently avoid danger? |
| An upgrade changes an action | Can I learn it safely before relying on it? |

These are decision prompts, not claims about guaranteed enemy behavior. Avoid borrowing a co-op tactic that depends on a teammate holding another position: solo play removes that assumption even when the underlying combat interaction exists.

## How can you judge whether a solo run improved?

Track an observable change rather than only how far you reached. Did you recover your position after a throw? Did you recognize a harmful combination of choices before buying it? Did you avoid repeating the same route into a corner? A run can teach one of these lessons even if its final result is worse.

Write down the encounter, equipment and condition that caused trouble. That gives you something concrete to compare with the next attempt, and it makes a useful correction report if an effect differs from this guide. We do not infer solo difficulty scaling, extra health or a separate unlock route from the advertised one-player support.
